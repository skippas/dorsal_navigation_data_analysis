For the test, trial number (experience) has a significant positive effect (slope) on the expected probability / proportion of correct decisions (pcorr). The slope for the relationship between trial and pcorr is 0.057 - that is, the log odds of a correct decision increases by 0.057 after each successive trial.

The predicted probability of a correct choice at the end of the test (0.941) was higher than at the start (trial 1) of the control 0.570 (95% CI: [0.373, 0.747]), and this difference between the end of test and start of control was significant (odds ratio = 12.089, p <0.001).

Also note that the pcorr for the test is estimated to be significantly above chance (95% confidence ribbon does not overlap the 50% line) for the entire duration of the test and by the end of the test phase (in the 47th trial) is estimated to be NA. In contrast, the pcorr in the control phase is never significantly above chance for the duration of the control (95% confidence ribbon overlaps the 50% line).

That the model suggests naive wasps perform above chance at the onset of the test phase (ie. in trial 1) can be attributed to the fact that the model is unable to fit the rapid learning of the wasps, rather than to the availability and learning of olfactory and / or temporal cues during the pre-test training phase. This is supported by the proportion of correct decisions in the first few 3-trial wide windows of the test phase (black points) that are clustered around 50%.

Just keeping it simple and making comparisons to the 50% line after different points in the experiment: different models (like those including day or not) might produce very different slope estimates for the effect of learning, so I want to avoid that complexity for now. Whereas the predictions of the probability of a correct decision after n trials (and days) changes much less I reckon.

To answer whether wasps require optic flow information to discriminate patterns we tested whether they could distinguish between oblique stripes oriented at 45 degrees to the right or left of the y-maze arm and the general direction of wasp travel. Wasps had no trouble distinguishing between oblique stripes that presumably produce identical rates of optic flow. Initial performance (at trial 1) was not significantly different from chance (pcorr = , 95% CI: ) but rose steeply, reaching 0.984403, NA, 0.8853961 (95% CI: 0.984403, NA, 0.8853961 ) by the end of the experiment, which is significantly higher than expected by chance (50%).

Next, we asked whether wasps could discriminate between an opaque and transparent roof, ie. between stimuli that carried little to no pattern information. Wasp performance was not significantly different from 50% (pcorr = 0.60955, 95% CI: [0.436, 0.759])) at the onset of the experiment (trial 1), but rose with experience and by trial 13 pcorr was 0.6985397 (95% CI: [0.581, 0.795]) and significantly different from 50%. The line of best fit suggests a continued increase in performance and a pcorr of 0.7804339 (95% CI: [0.500, 0.927] by the end of the experiment (trial 26). Performance at the end of the experiment is not significantly higher than 50% because of the increased uncertainty in the slope / effect of trials on performance due to the lower number of trials run in this experiment.
